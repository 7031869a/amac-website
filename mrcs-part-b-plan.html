Two hurdles, studied in order of what each is worth.

The book runs in teaching order. The exam does not. This sequences the stations in the station library (coming soon) by how many marks each one actually covers — and keeps Knowledge and Skills on separate tracks, because they are passed or failed separately and marks do not carry across.

Sequencing only. No clinical content is changed, ranked or omitted.

Why the plan is split. There is no overall pass mark for the OSCE. Candidates must pass Applied Knowledge (160 marks) and Applied Skills (180 marks) in a single sitting, and failing one does not let you bank the other. A strong section cannot compensate for a weak one.

So every session below carries both, and the marks each session covers are counted against its own section rather than against a single 340-mark total. How Part B is scored →

  • A slot’s marks per circuit divided by the number of stations that could fill it gives what each station studied is worth. Pathology sits at the top of Knowledge because 40 marks ride on a pool of four stations; anatomy sits at the bottom because 60 marks are spread over 23.
  • Within each section, sessions interleave slots rather than blocking them, so no sitting is spent entirely on prosections.
  • Marks per station studied are comparable within a section, not across the two. Twelve Skills marks and twelve Knowledge marks are not interchangeable, because the sections are passed separately.
  • This ranks revision order, not importance. Every station in the library is in the plan; none is dropped.
  • Appendix stations are excluded, since their presence in the current rotation is unconfirmed.
  • The 17-station, 340-mark structure and the domain allocations come from ICBSE’s Guide to the intercollegiate MRCS examination (August 2021); the two-hurdle rule from Standard Setting in the MRCS Part B (OSCE). Which book station fills which blueprint slot is AMaC’s mapping, not the Colleges’.
