# Verified current NICE positions (fetched directly from nice.org.uk on 8 Oct 2026)

## Sepsis — NG51 is WITHDRAWN. Replaced by three guidelines:
- NG253 Suspected sepsis in people aged 16 or over (published 19 Nov 2025, last updated 23 Sep 2026)
- NG254 Suspected sepsis in under 16s
- NG255 Suspected sepsis in pregnant or recently pregnant people

NG253 (aged 16+, not pregnant):
- Risk is stratified with NEWS2: high risk = NEWS2 7 or above (or below 7 with a single parameter scoring 3 confirmed
  by medical review as high risk, or other clinical concern); medium risk = NEWS2 5 or 6 (or similar single-parameter rule).
- 1.8.3 High risk: broad-spectrum IV antibiotics within 1 hour of calculating the NEWS2 score (ED initial assessment
  or ward deterioration). Take cultures/samples before antimicrobials (1.8.1).
- 1.8.17 Medium risk: an FY2-level-or-above clinician should consider deferring antibiotics for up to 3 hours after
  the first NEWS2 to gather information for a more specific diagnosis.
- Bloods for high risk include venous blood gas with glucose and lactate, cultures, FBC, CRP, U&E, creatinine, LFTs,
  clotting; consider procalcitonin [2026].
- 1.8.5 Fluid: isotonic crystalloid — a BALANCED solution such as Hartmann's, or 0.9% saline if balanced not available.
- 1.8.6–1.8.9 Volume: initial bolus 250 ml over 10–15 minutes; further 250 ml boluses if needed up to 1,000 ml total;
  reassess after each; if not improved after 1,000 ml, get senior clinical decision maker advice.
  (The old "500 ml bolus" for adults is OUTDATED for non-pregnant adults.)
NG254 (under 16): crystalloid with Na 130–154 mmol/L; bolus 10 ml/kg over <10 min, max 250 ml per bolus
  (neonates <28 days: 10–20 ml/kg). Antibiotics within 1 hour of meeting any high-risk criterion. Senior review
  within 3 hours for 2+ moderate-high risk criteria (age groups apply). (Old "20 ml/kg" for children is OUTDATED
  except the neonatal 10–20 ml/kg range.)
NG255 (pregnant or recently pregnant, 16+): crystalloid Na 130–154, bolus 500 ml over <15 min; reassess; second bolus
  if no improvement; consultant to attend if no improvement after the second. Antibiotics within 1 hour of meeting any
  high-risk criterion.
Note: lactate thresholds (e.g. "lactate over 4 = high risk") were NG51 criteria; in NG253 risk is NEWS2-based and
  lactate is part of the assessment, not the adult risk-stratification rule. Child/pregnancy guidelines keep
  criteria-based tables — check before calling anything outdated.
The UK Sepsis Trust "Sepsis Six" is a separate, non-NICE bundle — do not call it outdated on NICE grounds alone.

## Suspected cancer — NG12 (last updated 15 Apr 2026)
- Terminology: "suspected cancer pathway referral" = diagnosis or ruling out of cancer within 28 days (NHS England
  Faster Diagnosis Standard). NG12 no longer uses "2-week wait" / "appointment within 2 weeks". ("Urgent" for TESTS in
  NG12 still means "before 2 weeks"; "very urgent" = within 48 hours.)
  => A question that says "2-week-wait referral" is TERMINOLOGY (update wording), not clinically wrong, unless it turns
     on the 2-week timeframe itself.
- May 2025: 1.2.1 (oesophageal) and 1.2.7 (stomach): REFER using a suspected cancer pathway referral if dysphagia, OR
  aged 55+ with weight loss AND any of upper abdominal pain, reflux, dyspepsia. This REPLACED "offer urgent direct
  access upper GI endoscopy (within 2 weeks)". Non-urgent direct-access endoscopy remains for e.g. haematemesis (1.2.2/1.2.8)
  and some other groups.
- April 2026 ovarian (1.5.6–1.5.11):
  - aged 39 or under with persistent symptoms: do NOT use CA125 in isolation; consider an urgent direct-access
    ultrasound of abdomen and pelvis.
  - aged 40+: measure CA125 in primary care; arrange urgent direct-access USS abdomen/pelvis if CA125 meets the
    AGE-SPECIFIC threshold: 40–49 ≥35; 50–59 ≥31; 60–69 ≥24; 70–79 ≥25; 80+ ≥31 IU/ml.
  - USS suggests ovarian cancer → suspected cancer pathway referral to gynaecological cancer service.
  - CA125 below threshold, or USS normal → look for other causes; safety-net.
  - Unchanged: persistent symptoms list (bloating, early satiety/loss of appetite, pelvic/abdominal pain, urinary
    urgency/frequency, esp. ≥50, >12 times/month); ascites/pelvic or abdominal mass not obviously fibroids → urgent
    referral (1.5.1 — check exact text if relevant); new IBS-type symptoms aged 50+ → test.
  (The old single "CA125 ≥35 IU/ml → ultrasound" rule for all ages is OUTDATED except for age 40–49.)
- April 2026 endometrial: 1.5.12 aged 55+ with unexplained post-menopausal bleeding that CANNOT BE ATTRIBUTED TO HRT →
  suspected cancer pathway referral. 1.5.14 under 55 with unexplained PMB not attributable to HRT → CONSIDER pathway
  referral. 1.5.15 unscheduled bleeding on HRT → see British Menopause Society guidance. 1.5.13 (USS for 55+ with
  discharge etc.) unchanged.
