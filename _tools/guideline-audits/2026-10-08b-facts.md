# Verified current guidance (fetched from the official pages, 8 October 2026)

## 1. Folic acid before/in pregnancy — NICE NG247 Maternal and child nutrition (published 15 January 2025)
- 1.1.4 Advise 400 micrograms folic acid a day for anyone planning pregnancy or in the first 12 weeks.
- 1.1.5 OFFER 5 mg a day if increased risk of NTD/congenital malformation, for example: they (or partner) have, or family history
  of, NTD or other congenital malformation; previous pregnancy affected by NTD/congenital malformation; type 1 or type 2 diabetes;
  haematological condition needing folic acid (sickle cell, thalassaemia); medicines affecting folate absorption/metabolism
  (e.g. anti-epileptic medicines, medicines for HIV).
- 1.1.6 REASSURE anyone with BMI 25 kg/m² or more that they do NOT need more than 400 micrograms a day unless a 1.1.5 factor applies.
- 1.1.7 Same reassurance for increased risk of pre-eclampsia: 400 micrograms is enough unless a 1.1.5 factor applies.
- 1.1.8 After bariatric surgery: contact their bariatric unit for individualised advice.
- CONFLICT: RCOG GTG72 (Care of women with obesity in pregnancy, 2018) still says 5 mg for BMI ≥30. NICE NG247 is newer and is
  the national standard used for UKMLA/PLAB. A question that KEYS 5 mg folic acid because of obesity alone, or teaches
  "BMI ≥30 → 5 mg", is now outdated. Preferred fix: re-key to NICE (400 micrograms) and cite NG247, OR add a 1.1.5 factor
  (e.g. type 2 diabetes) to the stem so 5 mg is right for an undisputed reason. Do not leave a keyed answer that depends on
  the NICE/RCOG conflict. Epilepsy on antiepileptics, diabetes (type 1 OR type 2), sickle cell, thalassaemia, previous NTD,
  HIV medicines → 5 mg remains correct. Coeliac disease is NOT in NG247's list (not an error to mention as "for example",
  but don't key it on NG247 alone).

## 2. Pneumonia and CRP — NICE NG250 Pneumonia: diagnosis and management (published 2 September 2025)
- NG250 REPLACES CG191 (2014) and antimicrobial guidelines NG138 and NG139. Any citation of CG191/NG138/NG139 is now a
  withdrawn-guideline citation → "wording" fix to NG250.
- Point-of-care CRP for suspected LRTI when unclear if antibiotics needed now sits in NICE NG237 (Suspected acute respiratory
  infection in over 16s, published Oct 2023, last updated Sept 2025) 1.3.4: CRP >100 mg/L offer immediate antibiotics;
  20–100 mg/L consider back-up prescription; <20 mg/L do not routinely offer antibiotics. (Same thresholds as before —
  only the citation changes.)
- NG250 key points: CRB65 in primary care (refer to hospital at 2 or more; for 1 consider primary care-led services with
  safety-netting, or virtual ward / SDEC / hospital at home), CURB65 in hospital. Diagnosis incl. CXR and antibiotics within
  4 hours of presentation. Oral antibiotics first line if possible; IV review by 48 h. Adults with CAP: stop antibiotics after
  5 days unless micro results say otherwise or not clinically stable. NEW 2025: children 3 months–11 years with non-severe CAP
  without complications: 3-day course. NEW 2025: adults with HIGH-severity CAP in hospital — CONSIDER a corticosteroid
  (start with IV hydrocortisone) in addition to antibiotics for 4–7 days or until discharge (the old CG191 said do NOT routinely
  offer corticosteroids — a question keying "no steroids" for high-severity CAP is now outdated). No routine microbiological
  tests for low-severity CAP. Consider baseline CRP on admission; consider CRP or procalcitonin at day 3–4 if concern about
  treatment failure. Do not routinely offer follow-up CXR after discharge; consider at 6 weeks if risk factors for lung cancer
  or other respiratory disease (e.g. smokers, over 50) or persisting symptoms/weight loss.

## 3. Ovarian cysts in postmenopausal women — RCOG GTG34 (2016), amended December 2025
- Amendment (RCOG/RCR consensus, supported by BGCS and BSUR): unilateral, unilocular simple ovarian cysts measuring 3 cm or
  less on imaging in postmenopausal women do NOT require routine follow-up.
- Rest unchanged: assess with CA125 and transvaginal ultrasound, calculate RMI I; RMI ≥200 → gynaecological oncology MDT/
  referral (CT abdomen/pelvis); simple, unilateral, unilocular cyst >3 cm and <5 cm with normal CA125 → repeat USS and CA125
  in 4–6 months (conservative management); discharge if unchanged/resolved after 1 year. Larger/complex cysts → surgery.
- A question keying "repeat scan in 4–6 months" (or any routine follow-up) for a simple unilocular cyst ≤3 cm in a
  postmenopausal woman is now outdated. If the cyst is >3 cm and <5 cm it remains correct.
- Also check CA125 use against NG12 April 2026 age thresholds (40–49 ≥35; 50–59 ≥31; 60–69 ≥24; 70–79 ≥25; 80+ ≥31 IU/mL)
  where a question concerns suspected ovarian cancer referral in primary care.

## 4. VTE in pregnancy — RCOG Position Statement on GTG 37a (3 September 2026), aligned with NHS England Maternal Care Bundle
- Clinicians follow current GTG37a WITH these amendments; first trimester / pre-booking / miscarriage / termination → NHS
  England Maternal Care Bundle (MCB).
- Booking BMI ≥50 kg/m²: OFFER LMWH thromboprophylaxis throughout pregnancy and for 6 weeks postpartum (new threshold).
- Risk score changes (Appendix I): BMI 30–39 = 1; BMI 40–49 = 2; BMI ≥50 = 4. Transient factors now score 4: any surgical
  procedure in pregnancy/puerperium (except immediate perineal repair), OHSS, current systemic infection (requiring IV
  antibiotics or admission), immobility/dehydration. Hyperemesis = 3. Other scores as before (previous VTE 4; VTE provoked by
  surgery 3; high-risk thrombophilia 3; comorbidities 3; age >35, parity ≥3, smoker, gross varicose veins, family history 1;
  pre-eclampsia, IVF, multiple pregnancy 1; CS in labour 2; elective CS 1; mid-cavity/rotational birth 1; prolonged labour
  >24 h 1; PPH >1 L or transfusion 1; preterm <37 weeks 1; stillbirth 1).
- Thresholds unchanged: antenatal score ≥4 → consider prophylaxis from first trimester; score 3 → from 28 weeks; postnatal
  score ≥2 → at least 10 days; antenatal admission → consider; readmission in puerperium → consider.
- Admission with hyperemesis or another transient immobilising condition: LMWH for the duration of the risk and for a further
  week after it resolves (MCB: hyperemesis → offer LMWH within 72 hours).
- NEW WEIGHT-BASED DOSES (replace old GTG37a table):
    < 100 kg: enoxaparin 40 mg daily; dalteparin 5000 units; tinzaparin 4500 units
    100–129 kg: enoxaparin 60 mg daily; dalteparin 7500 units; tinzaparin 7000 units
    130–169 kg: enoxaparin 80 mg daily; dalteparin 10 000 units; tinzaparin 9000 units
    170+ kg: enoxaparin 0.6 mg/kg/day; dalteparin 75 units/kg/day; tinzaparin 75 units/kg/day
  OLD table (now superseded): <50 kg 20 mg; 50–90 kg 40 mg; 91–130 kg 60 mg; 131–170 kg 80 mg; >170 kg 0.6 mg/kg.
  So: 91–99 kg was 60 mg, is now 40 mg; 130 kg was 60 mg, is now 80 mg; <50 kg was 20 mg, is now 40 mg (pregnancy table).
- Source to cite: "RCOG Green-top Guideline No. 37a (2015) with RCOG Position Statement, September 2026".
