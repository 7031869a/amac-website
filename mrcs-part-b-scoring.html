← Part B contents MRCS Part B · How it is scored

Two hurdles, not one total.

The station book teaches the stations. It does not say how the exam is scored — and the scoring rule changes how you should revise. This is taken entirely from ICBSE’s own published documents.

Procedural reference, drawn from published sources and cited below.

The rule that matters most

There is no overall pass mark for the OSCE. Candidates must pass both sections — Applied Knowledge and Applied Skills — in a single sitting.

Failing one section does not let you bank the other and resit only the section you failed. Both are taken again.

That makes the two sections independent hurdles, and it has a direct consequence for revision: strength in one section does not compensate for weakness in the other. Marks are not pooled.

How the pass mark is set

Each station is assessed twice over: a mark for each domain using structured descriptors, and an overall judgement of the performance as a whole — pass, borderline or fail. So every station yields both a mark out of 20 and a global rating.

Those two together drive borderline regression: the station’s pass mark is derived from the relationship between the marks awarded and the global ratings, rather than being fixed in advance. A circuit’s pass mark is then compiled from the pass marks of its individual scenarios, and 0.84 SEM is added to each of Knowledge and Skills to hold the standard.

  • Scenario-level calculations draw on every UK sitting back to February 2013, capped at the most recent 500 uses of that scenario.
  • Only scenarios not significantly altered in that period are included.
  • Each circuit gets its own pass mark. Circuits differ slightly in difficulty, so the pass mark differs slightly between them — it is not a fixed percentage.
  • A circuit is identical across all UK and Ireland centres running on a given day, and may repeat on a later day in the same diet.

ICBSE notes that because both sections must be passed together, the effective SEM for the exam as a whole exceeds the 1.0 generally treated as a desirable minimum.

ICBSE, Standard Setting in the MRCS Part B (OSCE), which cites the Academy of Medical Royal Colleges standard-setting framework for the borderline regression method.

What actually happens on the day

  • Stations run 9 minutes each. You get one minute outside to read the instructions, which stay available inside the bay.
  • In physical examination and history-taking stations a signal comes at 6 minutes: present your findings from that point.
  • Some examined stations are preceded by a 9-minute preparation station. The Royal College of Surgeons of England describes a circuit of 19 nine-minute stations, with one or more rest stations.
  • The stations are split into two circuits with an interval of roughly 15 to 20 minutes between them.
  • Total duration is approximately 3 hours 30 minutes.
  • The circuit may include an additional, unidentified pilot station that does not count towards your mark.
  • Photographic ID is required; phones and electronic devices are handed in before the circuit; a dress code applies for infection control.
  • At the end there is a debrief, a feedback form, and an informal discussion with the Supervising Examiner.

Attempts

Part A must be passed before applying for Part B. Candidates are allowed up to six attempts at Part A and four attempts at Part B, irrespective of the interval between them; one further attempt may be granted under the Additional Attempt Policy. Part B must be passed within seven years of passing Part A — otherwise Part A has to be taken and passed again. That seven-year limit is an ICBSE regulation, not a GMC rule.

ICBSE, Regulations for the Intercollegiate MRCS Examination (January 2025), 3.10.1–3.10.3 and 4.2–4.3.

What this changes about revision

  • Balance across the two sections, not across the whole exam. Marks do not pool, so a strong Knowledge section cannot rescue a weak Skills section. The study plan in this pack sequences within each section for that reason.
  • The global rating is a second judgement, not a summary of the marks. A performance can accumulate marks and still read as borderline overall; the two are recorded separately.
  • There is no fixed percentage to aim at. The pass mark is generated per circuit, so the useful target is the standard described in the descriptors, not a number.
  • The 6-minute signal is a structural fact, not a suggestion. In examination and history stations, everything you have not elicited by then has to be presented as unfinished.
