Provenance and standards

How these stations map to the exam

  • The MRCS is set by the Intercollegiate Committee for Basic Surgical Examinations (ICBSE) on behalf of the four surgical Royal Colleges. It is not a GMC examination.
  • The MRCS syllabus sits within the GMC-approved curriculum for the early years of surgical training, and ICBSE states that the exam's marking domains are constructed so that the areas identified by the GMC's Good Medical Practice are adequately covered.
  • ICBSE marking domains: clinical knowledge and its application; clinical and technical skill; professionalism, including decision-making, problem solving, situational awareness, judgement and organisation; communication. Every station here is tagged with the domains it sits under.
  • Part B is an OSCE of 9-minute stations across anatomy and surgical pathology, applied surgical science and critical care, and clinical, procedural and communication skills.
  • Clinical content follows the station book, which carries its own dated source checks. Where a station touches consent, capacity or candour, the standard applied is current GMC professional guidance; specific risk figures are deliberately not asserted — candidates are directed to current national or local guidance.
